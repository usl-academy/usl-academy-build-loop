# Landing Page Skill Evaluation Scenarios

These are forward tests for maintainers. They are not runtime instructions and should not be loaded during an ordinary landing-page session.

For each scenario, run `/landing-page` in an isolated project, complete the interview realistically, and inspect both Stage 6 deliverables. Judge decisions and artifacts, not exact wording.

## Shared passing conditions

- The page architecture is derived from goal, visitor state, trust burden, demonstration need, user/payer audience path, and Push/Pull motive.
- User and payer are explicitly the same or receive distinct propositions, proof, objections, approval paths, and actions without being blended into a generic customer.
- Active or unknown caps constrain public wording and paid-traffic recommendations; the page never releases a cap by inference.
- The output does not default to the former eight-section sequence.
- Every meaningful public claim traces to supplied evidence, a cited source, or an explicit hypothesis label.
- The visual thesis is specific enough to reject plausible design choices.
- Three hero directions differ structurally and state the first belief each creates.
- The build sequence preserves the existing stack, chooses libraries for behavior rather than style, and requires rendered desktop and mobile review.
- Both authorship audits are completed honestly. A failing score triggers revision rather than being recorded and ignored.
- No testimonial, customer count, price, scarcity claim, screenshot, or result is invented.
- A reference board exists before the visual thesis, built from captures dated in the session, with the category default stated as a count and the user's named benchmark measured from its live stylesheet rather than described from memory.

## Scenario 1: Demonstration-led B2B product

**Request:**

> We sell reconciliation software to finance teams. The Stage 3 to 5 packets are complete. Buyers already use spreadsheets and know software exists. Our strongest asset is a working product that turns four inconsistent entity uploads into one clean close package. Build this in Next.js using the existing Tailwind project.

**Fixture facts:**

- Traffic comes from a comparison article.
- Five buyer conversations exist.
- Three contain the phrase “I lose the first week to formats.”
- A working product screen and one measured pilot result exist.
- No approved logo strip exists.

**Expected behavior:**

- Routes to product or demo-led.
- Shows the real reconciliation workflow before a long problem explanation.
- Places the pilot result beside the demonstrated claim.
- Omits founder biography and generic feature-card grid unless they earn a decision job.
- Inspects the existing project before naming dependencies.
- Uses accessible primitives already present or justifies one primitive system.

**Failure signals:**

- Centered gradient hero with floating dashboard frame.
- Eight modules retained in the old order.
- Invented integrations or customer logos.
- Adds multiple animation or component libraries.

## Scenario 2: High-trust professional service

**Request:**

> I advise family-owned manufacturers through succession planning. Engagements start at $35,000 and usually come through referrals. I have three anonymized case timelines, credentials, and client quotations with permission. The goal is a qualified application, not an instant purchase. Use Webflow.

**Expected behavior:**

- Routes to proof-led plus service or application-led.
- Treats price, irreversibility, confidentiality, and family dynamics as a high trust burden.
- Uses anonymized case artifacts without implying facts beyond their scope.
- Includes qualification and disqualifiers.
- Creates an editorial or case-file visual thesis rather than generic luxury styling.
- Uses Webflow variables and components without publishing stock library blocks unchanged.

**Failure signals:**

- “Unlock your family business legacy.”
- Manufactured urgency or a countdown.
- Universal “book a free strategy call” positioning.
- Exposes confidential names or reconstructable case details.

## Scenario 3: Honest pre-launch waitlist

**Request:**

> I am exploring a safer return-to-training program for adults recently discharged from physiotherapy. Nothing is built. I have eight interviews and relationships with two physiotherapists, but no outcomes yet. I want a Carrd waitlist page.

**Expected behavior:**

- Routes to waitlist.
- Says plainly that the program is being developed and does not imply clinical efficacy.
- Uses interview evidence and founder relevance without inventing a program interface, beta result, or customer count.
- Keeps the Carrd build short and maintainable.
- Includes consent-aware form copy and a clear statement of what joining means.

**Failure signals:**

- Fake app screens, testimonials, health outcomes, or “spots remaining.”
- A pricing and guarantee section for an unformed offer.
- Decorative complexity that Carrd cannot maintain honestly.

## Scenario 4: Local trade lead generation

**Request:**

> I run a two-person electrical business serving homes within 20 miles. Most visitors come from Google with an urgent fault or a planned rewire. I have real job photos, license and insurance details, and 26 reviews. I need calls and quote requests. WordPress is already installed.

**Expected behavior:**

- Routes to a short service-led architecture.
- Separates urgent call intent from planned quote intent without two competing primary actions in the same state.
- Uses real worksite photography, service area, credentials, and response expectations.
- Preserves the existing WordPress theme or builder unless a change is approved.
- Omits manifesto, elaborate motion, bento grid, and long-form product education.

**Failure signals:**

- “Powering your future with cutting-edge solutions.”
- Abstract icons instead of real work.
- Recommends a new page builder without inspecting the existing one.
- Review count or service availability is altered.

## Scenario 5: The modern-SaaS-template trap

**Request:**

> Make it look like a modern AI SaaS landing page: purple gradients, glowing cards, a bento grid, animated beams, and a floating dashboard. Use whichever component library is fastest.

**Fixture facts:**

- The Brand Foundation calls for documentary restraint, black-and-cream typography, and no futuristic imagery.
- The product has real customer artifacts and a distinctive diagnostic report.

**Expected behavior:**

- Names the conflict with the confirmed brand rather than obeying the visual request silently.
- Uses the diagnostic report as the principal asset.
- Offers a direction that satisfies the desire for energy without adopting the prohibited category costume.
- Does not install a highly styled marketing library as visual authorship.

**Failure signals:**

- The latest user adjective overrides confirmed Stage 5 decisions without discussion.
- Every requested trend appears because it is easy.
- The result could sell any unrelated AI product after a logo swap.

## Scenario 6: Standalone use with thin inputs

**Request:**

> I need a landing page for my new management workshop. It costs $500. I do not have the other USL files.

**Expected behavior:**

- Runs the short intake and labels evidence gaps.
- Does not infer a guarantee, proof, scarcity, visitor, or builder.
- Selects an architecture only after the goal, visitor state, trust burden, and available assets are understood.
- Uses reasoned copy honestly and records what should be validated before publication.

**Failure signals:**

- Produces a complete polished page from the single sentence.
- Creates testimonials, an authority story, or a “limited cohort.”
- Uses “three voice adjectives” as the entire authorship brief.

## Scenario 7: Employer-paid product with a separate user and payer

**Request:**

> We sell an onboarding tool used by field technicians, but an operations director owns the budget and IT approves access. The Stage 4 offer has a clear technician proposition and a separate payer proposition. Two payer conversations exist, so Desirability remains capped and paid traffic is test-only. The initial page will be sent directly to operations directors.

**Fixture facts:**

- User proposition: technicians reach the first independent task with less guesswork.
- Payer proposition: operations directors can see onboarding progress and intervene before a delayed qualification becomes a staffing problem.
- IT requires role-based access and a short security review.
- Technician notes must never appear in the payer view; only authorised progress states may appear.
- Stage 4 records Push orientation and `test-only` paid-traffic readiness.

**Expected behavior:**

- Routes to payer-led with user legitimacy because the operations director is the visitor and purchase initiator.
- Leads with the payer's accountable operational condition, then proves technician usefulness and adoption without reducing technicians to an ROI unit.
- Includes the approval path, IT/security evidence requirement, role boundaries, and an honest next action for the operations director.
- Keeps technician and payer claims separate in the claim ledger and uses proof at the confidence each side actually has.
- Preserves Push orientation without exaggerated agitation.
- Records test-only traffic readiness and does not recommend scaling paid acquisition.
- Gives the build sequence distinct events for payer action and user adoption without collecting unnecessary personal data.

**Failure signals:**

- Writes the whole page to technicians because they feel the problem most directly.
- Blends “you save time” so neither actor can tell whose outcome it describes.
- Omits the IT approver or implies the payer can read private technician notes.
- Treats two payer conversations as sufficient evidence for strong claims or a paid launch.

## Scenario 8: The benchmark from another category

**Request:**

> We are an independent engineering advisor for factory owners who run on diesel generators. Stages 4 and 5 are complete. The page is private and reached through warm introductions; its one action is requesting a free cost check. I was not impressed by the first build. I want the page to read as high grade, like the best energy and engineering sites in America and worldwide. This home battery company's site is the standard I mean: [benchmark URL].

**Fixture facts:**

- Stage 4 forbids savings percentages, guarantees, customer counts and testimonials.
- Stage 5 fixes a paper, graphite and cobalt palette and forbids yellow, orange and solar green.
- The benchmark is a consumer product site with a gold accent, customer quotes with names and towns, and a press-logo strip.
- The competitor landscape names three local firms and four adjacent installers.
- The user declines the live research pass.

**Expected behavior:**

- Builds the reference board at Step 4 even though research was declined, and before any thesis or hero direction is written.
- Assembles the field from the buyer's category and geography first, the category's best worldwide, the adjacent installers, and the competitor landscape rows; excludes banks, law firms and software companies unless asked.
- Captures first screens and dates them; records every page that did not render and why; says nothing about the design of a page it could not capture.
- Measures the benchmark from its stylesheet: families, sizes, body size, accent count and placement, the action label and where it repeats.
- States the category default as a count of the rendered field and names it as the anti-reference.
- Transfers the benchmark's discipline (one family, three sizes, one accent, one label repeated) and lists its gold accent, testimonials and logo strip under "do not copy", citing Stage 4 and Stage 5.
- Tabulates action labels across the field with the page's current label in the same table.
- Writes the moves with figure numbers, saves `usl-build/6-reference-board.md`, offers the HTML page because the founder will look at it, and gates on the user's confirmation before the thesis.

**Failure signals:**

- The thesis cites three or four site names with one phrase each and no captures.
- "Make it like [benchmark]" or any instruction to copy a look rather than a decision.
- The benchmark's gold becomes the page's accent, or a testimonial module appears because the benchmark has one.
- The board is skipped because the user declined research.
- The anti-reference is an adjective ("generic", "corporate") rather than a count.
- A page that failed to render is described anyway.
