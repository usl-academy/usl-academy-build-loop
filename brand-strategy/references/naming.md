# Naming Systems and Preliminary Screening

Read this file only for Step 8, the Brand Naming Sprint, or the Offer Naming Sprint.

## 1. Preliminary name screening

Naming strategy proposes candidates. It does not provide legal clearance. Before calling any brand, offer, or lead-magnet name selected, separate creative fit from availability risk.

Screen the shortlist, not every raw candidate:

1. **Exact and confusingly similar use.** Search the exact phrase, close spellings, phonetic equivalents, and the same root words in the relevant category and geography. A result outside the category may still matter if audiences or channels overlap.
2. **Relevant trademark sources.** Check the official trademark database for the operating jurisdiction when live research is approved and the source is accessible. Record classes or categories inspected and obvious similar marks. State plainly that this is preliminary screening, not a legal opinion or comprehensive clearance search.
3. **Domains and handles.** Check only the domains and social platforms the user actually expects to use. Exact `.com` availability is one input, not an automatic veto; confusing workarounds and misleading handles are stronger warnings.
4. **Language and pronunciation.** Test likely pronunciation, spelling from hearing, unintended meanings, and awkward transliterations in the brand's operating languages. Do not claim a comprehensive linguistic review without a qualified speaker.
5. **Category comprehension.** Ask whether a relevant buyer can tell what kind of thing this is, or whether the surrounding descriptor can do that work. Mystery is not the same as distinctiveness.
6. **Memory and transmission.** Test whether someone can remember the name, say it, type it, and recommend it after one exposure.
7. **Future fit.** Check whether the name can stretch to planned offers, audiences, or geographies without becoming misleading.

Use one of these statuses:

- **Screened for exploration:** no obvious conflict found in the checks performed; legal clearance may still be needed.
- **Needs specialist review:** a similar mark, regulated context, linguistic uncertainty, or high-cost launch makes professional review prudent.
- **Rejected:** an obvious conflict, misleading implication, voice failure, or practical usability problem makes the candidate not worth advancing.
- **Unverified:** live screening was declined, unavailable, or incomplete. Never describe an unverified name as cleared, available, or final.

Record the source, URL, jurisdiction or platform, date checked, query or class where useful, finding, and status. Never imply that domain registration, a search-engine result, or a trademark database lookup alone creates rights.

## 2. Brand Naming Sprint

Use this system to name the brand itself. Do not use MAGIC, which is designed for offers.

### Naming brief

Capture only inputs that change the choice:

- Entity type and brand architecture: standalone, masterbrand, endorsed, sub-brand, or personal brand
- Founding conviction and Brand Idea when one exists
- Audience architecture and decision-maker
- Category, alternatives, and desired positioning
- Desired associations and associations to avoid
- Brand personality and voice register
- Existing recognition, words, initials, founder names, or equity worth preserving
- Future offers, categories, languages, and geographies the name must stretch to
- Legal, regulatory, cultural, pronunciation, length, or channel constraints

### Naming territories

Create three meaningfully different conceptual territories before generating names. A territory is a strategic source of meaning, not a word style. Examples include the founding belief, the transformation the user experiences, a distinctive method or metaphor, or the relationship the brand creates.

For each territory:

1. State the strategic idea in one sentence.
2. Explain why it belongs to this brand and not merely the category.
3. Generate 4 to 6 candidates using varied constructions: coined, suggestive, compound, metaphorical, founder-led, or descriptive where appropriate.
4. Remove generic category language, difficult pronunciations, and candidates that contradict the intended architecture before showing them.

Do not generate arbitrary misspellings merely to obtain a domain. Do not use culturally specific words without understanding their meaning and context.

### Brand-name scorecard

Score finalists from 1 to 5. The score supports judgment; it does not make the decision.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Strategic fit | Does it express the founding conviction, positioning, or Brand Idea? |
| Distinctiveness | Is it recognizably different without relying on invented exclusivity? |
| Audience fit | Does it sound credible and appealing to the intended user and payer? |
| Voice fit | Does it belong inside the chosen personality and register? |
| Ease | Can people pronounce, spell, remember, and share it? |
| Category navigation | Can buyers understand it directly or with a short descriptor? |
| Extensibility | Can it support planned offers, audiences, and geographies? |
| Screening risk | Did preliminary collision, trademark-source, language, and channel checks reveal problems? |

Weight strategic fit, audience fit, and screening risk more heavily than taste. Reject any candidate with a material screening problem regardless of its total.

## 3. Offer Naming: MAGIC

MAGIC is a construction method for offer and lead-magnet names. A name rarely uses all five components, and saying what was omitted is part of the work:

- **M — Magnet:** the reason someone stops and looks.
- **A — Avatar:** who the named offer is meant to move. When payer and user differ, follow the confirmed audience architecture rather than defaulting to the person receiving the service. The identity axis may be field, specialty, vertical, life stage, or geography.
- **G — Goal:** the decision-maker's desired outcome in their own words.
- **I — Interval:** the time frame.
- **C — Container:** the bundling word, such as kit, system, sprint, audit, programme, or method.

### Decision rules

- **Attach an interval only to something the business controls.** Pairing a quantified outcome with a duration implies a guarantee and may create platform or legal risk. Put the interval on a reliable deliverable or omit it.
- **Use only an interval the process reliably hits.** If Stage 4 says none is reliable, do not invent one.
- **Filter container words through the personality and register.** Aggressive containers can undermine professional, premium, or anxiety-sensitive offers. Reject rather than force.
- **Split the register deliberately.** A lead magnet can be bolder because it asks for attention; a core offer is read at a higher-risk purchase moment, where restraint may signal competence.
- **Check current advertising policy** before using a name in paid distribution.
- **Every candidate ladders to the Brand Idea.** A high-performing construction that contradicts the brand is still wrong.
- **Screen the shortlist.** Check exact and similar uses in the category, relevant trademark sources, language and pronunciation, and the domains or handles that materially affect launch.

Natural annual cohort turnover can refresh a market without frequent renaming. Do not rotate names merely because generic marketing advice says to.

## 4. Display-name sweep

Stage 4 uses plain working labels as stable identifiers for the packet, PRD, and build sequence. Stage 5 may change their presentation, never their identity.

For every customer-facing component:

- Write an outcome-forward, register-safe display name.
- Prefer a light lift over reinvention.
- Use `Unchanged` when the working label already fits.
- Keep the display name literal enough that the buyer can match it to what they receive.
- Record the working-label-to-display-name pair. Stage 6 uses the display name; implementation documents retain the working label.

## 5. Brand-first offer fit

Before naming a new offer under an existing foundation, recommend a brand-extension sprint when:

- The offer serves a different person rather than a narrower slice of the same audience.
- The user-payer relationship or audience architecture materially changes.
- The offer's register conflicts with the foundation's voice.
- The category sits outside the territory established by the foundation.
- The founding conviction cannot honestly cover the new offer.

One signal requires a conversation. Two or more usually indicate a sub-brand or brand extension. In that sprint, use the parent foundation as a Step 0 input and record which voice governs which expression.

For an inherited foundation, add this directly beneath the packet header:

```markdown
**Origin:** Inherited from the [parent brand name] Brand Foundation ([source project or file], carried forward unchanged on [date]). Offer Naming below was written by the Offer Naming Sprint on [date] against that foundation's Brand Idea, tone of voice, and register constraint.
```

If the parent foundation changes later, inherited projects do not update automatically. Re-run the offer fit and naming work where it matters.
