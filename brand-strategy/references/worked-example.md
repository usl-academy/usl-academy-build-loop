# Worked Example: Groundwork

Read this file only when the user asks for an example or remains stuck after receiving tailored suggestions. It demonstrates the level of reasoning, not wording to copy.

**Brand:** Groundwork  
**Category:** Productivity coaching for early-career professionals  
**Context:** A founder who spent ten years in corporate strategy now coaches first-generation professionals on navigating the unwritten rules of corporate life.

## Discovery

**Core belief:** The professional world was built on networks and knowledge that not everyone inherited, and that gap is neither fair nor inevitable.  
**Tension:** Talented people from non-traditional backgrounds underperform their potential because nobody taught them how the game is played.  
**Founding conviction:** If you understand the rules, you can change them, but you have to learn them first.

## Definition and architecture

Groundwork is a standalone career-development and coaching brand for first-generation professionals. The name is already established, so no Brand Naming Sprint runs.

## Audience

**Demographics:** 24 to 34, two to eight years into a corporate career, college-educated, and often first in the family to work in that environment.  
**Behavior:** Consume career content, avoid asking for help openly, rarely negotiate, and tend to over-deliver and under-advocate.  
**Psychographics:** High achievers who believe hard work should speak for itself and worry they are missing knowledge others inherited.  
**Motivations and needs:** Want practical tools and belonging without assimilation.

**User:** Talented professionals who work harder than most but were never shown how advancement actually works.  
**Payer:** Same as user for the initial direct-to-consumer offer.  
**Audience architecture:** User-led; no separate payer route is needed.

## Positioning

**Category:** Professional coaching for early-career corporate employees.  
**Alternatives:** Generic LinkedIn career coaches, business-school alumni networks, and self-help books.

**Axes:**

- X: inspirational (`-2`) to practical (`+2`)
- Y: generic advice (`-2`) to insider knowledge (`+2`)

| Alternative | X | Y | Evidence basis | Confidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Groundwork | +2 | +2 | Intended strategy and curriculum | High |
| Generic career coaches | 0 | -1 | Category observation, not live checked | Low |
| Alumni networks | +1 | +1 | Format and access model | Medium |
| Self-help books | -1 | -1 | Broad category observation | Low |

This map is a hypothesis until competitor positioning is checked live. It does not claim Groundwork is the only practical source of insider knowledge.

**Positioning statement:** For first-generation professionals two to eight years into a corporate career, Groundwork is the coaching practice that translates the unwritten rules of advancement into practical tools, grounded in a former strategist's first-hand operating experience.

**Point of Difference:** Practical translation of hidden advancement norms for first-generation professionals, delivered without asking them to assimilate.

## Brand House

**Purpose:** This brand exists to level the playing field for people who were never given the playbook.  
**Audience insight:** First-generation professionals are not behind because they lack talent; they were never taught the invisible curriculum of corporate life.  
**Market insight:** Much career content offers inspiration without specific action. This remains a hypothesis until current competitor and audience evidence is checked.  
**Promise:** You will always leave a Groundwork session knowing exactly what to do next.  
**Values:** Specificity over inspiration. Belonging without assimilation. Results that can be observed.  
**Personality:** Direct, warm, pragmatic, credible, unsentimental.

## Brand Ladder

| Level | Content |
|---|---|
| Product Attributes | One-to-one coaching, an unwritten-rules curriculum, advocacy frameworks, and insider language guides |
| Functional Benefits | Navigate reviews, ask clearly, build senior relationships, and make work visible |
| User Benefits | Stop feeling like an outsider and act with informed confidence |
| Emotional End Benefit | Belonging on one's own terms |

**Brand Idea:** **Earned Belonging**

## Manifesto

Nobody handed you the playbook. You figured out most of it on your own. Still, other people seem to know things nobody explained to you. That gap is not a personal failure. It is a structural one, and it is fixable. Groundwork teaches the conversations, moves, and language that usually remain hidden. Not so you can become someone else. So you can stop holding back the person you already are.

## Identity direction

**Constraints:** The identity must work in small social avatars, coaching documents, slides, and a simple website. Body text must remain highly readable, and color cannot carry meaning alone. No existing visual equity needs preserving.

Three routes were considered: institutional playbook, warm insider, and editorial field notes. **Warm insider** was selected because it expresses credibility without reproducing the cold exclusivity the brand opposes.

**Logo:** A clear wordmark with a compact secondary mark for small spaces.  
**Color:** Deep navy and warm off-white with an amber accent, subject to contrast testing.  
**Typography:** A practical humanist sans serif for interfaces and headings, paired with a readable serif only where it remains accessible.  
**Imagery:** Real people doing real work. Quiet confidence, never performed success or stock-photo perfection.  
**Voice:** Direct, warm, specific. A trusted insider, not a motivational speaker.  
**Behavior:** Say the specific thing. Treat the client as capable of handling the truth. Never perform empathy without offering a tool.

## Offer naming

**Inputs:** The goal is to know what to do in the room. The identity axis is career stage plus first-generation status. No advancement interval is reliable. The first session can reliably produce a specific next move. The audience rejects performed inspiration and hype.

**Offer:** **The Unwritten Rules Programme**  
MAGIC uses Magnet and Container. Avatar and Goal remain implicit. Interval is omitted because advancement is not controlled by the business.

**Lead magnet:** **The Meeting You Are Getting Wrong**  
Its bolder register is appropriate for an attention exchange and remains specific rather than hyped.

**Rejected:**

- `Earned Belonging Accelerator`: turns the Brand Idea into a generic product label and uses an off-register container.
- `The Corporate Playbook`: crowded category language and weak distinctiveness.
- `Belong in 90 Days`: attaches time to an outcome the business does not control.

**Preliminary screening:** Not run in this example. Both names remain `Unverified`; they cannot be described as available or cleared.
